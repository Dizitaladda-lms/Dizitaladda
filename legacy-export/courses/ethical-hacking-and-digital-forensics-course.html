What is Ethical Hacking and Digital Forensics?

Ethical hacking is the authorised practice of testing computer systems, networks, and applications for security vulnerabilities using the same tools and techniques as malicious attackers — but with explicit permission, the goal of finding and fixing weaknesses before they can be exploited. Digital forensics is the complementary investigative discipline: identifying, preserving, collecting, and analysing digital evidence from computers, mobile devices, and networks after a breach or cybercrime, in a legally defensible way. DizitalAdda Ethical Hacking & Digital Forensics course covers both disciplines — the offensive and investigative skills that together represent the complete security analyst workflow — in a single 6-month programme across 6 modules and 10 live projects. What is VAPT and why is it included in an ethical hacking and forensics course?

VAPT (Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Testing) is the professional methodology that combines automated vulnerability scanning (using Nessus and OpenVAS to discover known weaknesses) with active penetration testing (using Metasploit to confirm and exploit those weaknesses). It bridges ethical hacking and forensics: after a penetration test, forensic techniques are used to document evidence, reconstruct what was accessed, and produce a formal report. DizitalAdda dedicated VAPT module covers the complete engagement workflow — scanning, exploitation, web app testing against the OWASP Top 10, and professional remediation reporting — making this course directly relevant to VAPT Analyst and Penetration Tester job roles. What is memory forensics and what tools are used to practise it?

Memory forensics is the analysis of a computer RAM (volatile memory) dump to recover running processes, open network connections, encryption keys, user credentials, and malware artefacts that exist only in memory and are never written to disk — making it a critical technique for investigating sophisticated attacks and fileless malware. In DizitalAdda Ethical Hacking & Digital Forensics course, memory forensics is taught as a dedicated 12-hour project (Project 8 — Memory Forensics & Incident Response Simulation) using Volatility, the industry-standard open-source memory analysis framework, to identify malicious processes and correlate them with log evidence for a complete incident response report. Call +91-8810606010.

What is the OWASP Top 10 and is it covered in this course?

The OWASP Top 10 is the Open Web Application Security Projects list of the ten most critical and most commonly exploited web application security vulnerabilities — including SQL injection, cross-site scripting (XSS), broken authentication, insecure direct object references (IDOR), and security misconfigurations. It is the industry-standard framework for web application penetration testing. DizitalAdda Ethical Hacking & Digital Forensics course covers the OWASP Top 10 as part of both the Ethical Hacking module and the dedicated VAPT module, practised hands-on using Burp Suite and SQLmap in a 15-hour web application penetration testing lab. What is chain of custody and why is it taught in this course?

Chain of custody is the documented, unbroken record of who collected, handled, transported, and stored a piece of digital evidence, and when each step occurred — a legal requirement that ensures evidence has not been tampered with and is admissible in court or formal organisational proceedings. It is taught in DizitalAdda Ethical Hacking & Digital Forensics course because VAPT analysts and forensics investigators increasingly need to produce legally defensible evidence documentation alongside technical findings reports — particularly as Indian regulatory bodies (CERT-In, RBI, SEBI) mandate more formal breach reporting. The chain of custody is practised in Project 7 (Disk Imaging & Evidence Analysis) and the Digital & Cyber Forensic Investigation module. Call +91-8810606010.

What is OSINT and how is it used in ethical hacking and forensics?

OSINT (Open-Source Intelligence) is the practice of gathering information about a target entirely from publicly available sources — including domain registration records, exposed subdomains, employee email addresses, and internet-connected device listings on Shodan — without ever touching the target systems directly. In ethical hacking, OSINT is the first phase of every professional penetration test (the footprinting or reconnaissance phase). In digital forensics, OSINT is used to trace cybercriminal activity and map attacker infrastructure. Project 9 of DizitalAdda Ethical Hacking & Digital Forensics course is a dedicated OSINT lab using Shodan and TheHarvester. Call +91-8810606010.
